# Soul of the journal

How to write an entry here.

## Voice

Write in first-person plural. "Today we attempted, we realized, we built."

It should read like you're telling a colleague over lunch what happened.
Plainspoken. Specific. Not a postmortem.

## Order

Tell the story forward, in the order things happened.

Show realizations as they unfolded — "as we build this, we realize..." —
not as conclusions you arrived at later.

## Shape

Short sentences. One or two thoughts each.

Break paragraphs into small chunks. A wall of text is the wrong shape
for a journal.

Avoid stacking em-dashes, parentheticals, and "while" clauses inside one
sentence. If you need a connector, start a new sentence.

## What goes in

- What we attempted today.
- What we realized along the way.
- What we built specifically — name the components.
- What that lets us do next.

## What stays out

- Editorial adjectives. "Quietly enforced", "the hard way", "real lesson".
- Lesson-for-the-next-engineer wrap-ups. The story is enough.
- Punchlines that require you to reorder events. Don't.
- Postmortem framing. This is a builder's notebook, not an incident review.

## Format

```
# YYYY-MM-DD

Today we [attempted | designed | built | realized] ...

[short chunks]
```

Keep the whole entry to roughly a paragraph, broken up. Two short
paragraphs is fine. Long enough to be substantive, short enough to read
in thirty seconds.
